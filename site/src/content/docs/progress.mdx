## A suggested order

The acts build strictly on each other, so the reading order is the learning order. Where you have a choice:

- **Do the lessons before the transfer layers.** Each act ends with *Test yourself* (recall from memory), *Diagnose it* (symptom-first drills that break your machine for real), and *In the wild* (the same ideas on your own Mac). They only work once the lessons are behind you.
- **Don't skip *Diagnose it*.** It's the closest thing here to the actual job, and it's the part most people skip.
- **The `b` and `c` lessons are on the main path**, not appendices — `01b`, `02b`, `03b`, `03c` sit where they do because that's when the question arises. The one genuine side road is *The container's filesystem*, which says so at the top.

## How review works

Most lessons carry a **Check yourself** box: a question with the answer folded away. Open the answer and it asks you to grade yourself — *Got it* or *Not yet*. That grade is the only thing that schedules anything.

A missed question comes back the next day; one you got comes back after ten days. Both then appear in **Due for review** above, missed ones first. That's the whole mechanism — no streaks, no daily target, nothing to keep up with. The gap between attempts is what makes the second one worth anything, so a queue that's empty most of the time is the queue working.
